# 04 — Is the bookmaker odds archive good enough to be our benchmark, and can we join it?

Type: research
Status: resolved

## Question

The destination requires beating the **closing line** on held-out seasons. That makes the odds archive load-bearing: if it's thin, mis-joined, or unusable, the entire validation target collapses.

**Part A — the archive itself.**
`tennis-data.co.uk` is the known free source (ATP from 2000, WTA from 2007, multiple books incl. Pinnacle and Bet365, weekly per-tournament CSV/Excel). Establish:
- Actual year and tour coverage today, including whether Challenger/ITF appear at all
- Which bookmakers, and whether **closing** odds are recorded or only opening
- Whether both max and average across books are given
- Its licence and terms — it's owned by "Tennis Data Innovations (UK) Ltd", a name that overlaps with the ATP's own rights JV. Establish whether these are the same entity, since that changes the terms materially
- Any alternative free odds archives worth knowing (Kaggle mirrors, oddsportal scrapes, Betfair historical exchange data — the exchange is arguably a *better* benchmark than bookmaker prices since it has no overround)

**Part B — the join, which is the hard part.**
Odds archives and match archives use different player-name conventions ("Djokovic N." vs "Novak Djokovic" vs a Sackmann player ID), different tournament names, and different date conventions. Establish:
- What the realistic join key is, and what match rate to expect
- Whether any existing crosswalk/mapping table is published
- How other public tennis-modelling projects have solved it — name repos and read their code

**Part C — the overround.**
Bookmaker prices include a margin. Establish the standard methods for removing it (proportional, Shin, logarithmic, power) and which the tennis modelling literature prefers for two-outcome markets. Getting this wrong makes any "we beat the market" claim meaningless.

**Deliverable:** a verdict on whether closing-line benchmarking is viable for ATP + WTA (and whether Challenger/ITF has any odds coverage at all), the concrete join strategy with an expected match rate, and a recommended de-vigging method with justification.

## Answer

*(Researched 2026-08-27. All tennis-data.co.uk claims below were verified against live downloads of the actual site, not against memory of it — file sizes, headers and row counts quoted are from real files fetched today.)*

### Part A — the archive itself

**What was actually fetched.** `curl` pulled `http://www.tennis-data.co.uk/2024/2024.xlsx` (445,729 bytes) and `http://www.tennis-data.co.uk/2024w/2024.xlsx` (406,783 bytes) directly — these are today's live files, `Last-Modified` headers and the site's own "Data Updated: 24th August 2026" banner confirm the archive is actively maintained through the current week, not a stale snapshot. `notes.txt` (the site's own data dictionary, also fetched live) and `data.php` (the site's own "about" page) were fetched too.

**Real column layout — ATP 2024 (`2024/2024.xlsx`), 36 columns, 2,703 rows:**
```
ATP, Location, Tournament, Date, Series, Court, Surface, Round, Best of,
Winner, Loser, WRank, LRank, WPts, LPts,
W1,L1,W2,L2,W3,L3,W4,L4,W5,L5, Wsets, Lsets, Comment,
B365W, B365L, PSW, PSL, MaxW, MaxL, AvgW, AvgL
```
**Real column layout — WTA 2024 (`2024w/2024.xlsx`), 32 columns, 2,490 rows:** identical shape, minus the 4th/5th-set game columns and with `Tier` instead of `Series`.

This is the single most important fact in the ticket, so stated plainly: **only two individual bookmakers survive in current files — Bet365 (`B365W`/`B365L`) and Pinnacle (`PSW`/`PSL`)** — plus `MaxW`/`MaxL` (max odds across books, per oddsportal.com) and `AvgW`/`AvgL` (average across books). `notes.txt` documents nine *other* bookmaker column pairs (Bet&Win, Centrebet, Expekt, Ladbrokes, Gamebookers, Interwetten, Sportingbet, Stan James, Unibet) that appeared in older seasons' files but that these bookmakers have since closed, merged, or stopped supplying odds, and their columns are simply absent from recent years. **A loader must treat the odds schema as year-dependent, not fixed** — don't hardcode a 36-column ATP schema across the full 2000–2026 range.

**Coverage today, verified against the live files:**
- ATP: results from **January 2000**, odds from **2001** (WTA/ATP option list on `alldata.php` runs 2000→2026 for ATP, with 2000 explicitly marked "(Match results)" only — no odds that first year). Confirmed the year links exist by parsing the raw HTML of `alldata.php`.
- WTA: results **and** odds both start at **2007** (`data.php`: "And from 2007 results and historical betting odds for the women's WTA tour as well!"). The oldest linked WTA file is `2007w/2007.xls`.
- Series/Tier values actually present in the 2024 files: ATP = `{ATP250, ATP500, Masters 1000, Masters Cup, Grand Slam}`; WTA = `{WTA250, WTA500, WTA1000, Tour Championships, Grand Slam}`. **No Challenger or ITF level appears anywhere** — confirmed both by the absence of any Challenger/ITF section in `alldata.php`'s tournament index (only a generic outbound nav link to `itftennis.com`, not a data section) and by the literal set of Series/Tier values in the downloaded rows. tennis-data.co.uk does not archive Challenger/ITF at all, full stop.
- Coverage completeness within the main tour: empirically, in ATP 2024 only 7/2,703 matches (0.26%) are missing Bet365 odds and 12/2,703 (0.44%) missing Pinnacle; WTA 2024 is similar (6/2,490 and 16/2,490). So within ATP/WTA main tour, odds coverage is >99.5% complete — the archive is not "thin" at the level it does cover.

**Closing vs. opening odds.** There is no separate opening/closing column pair (no `PSCW`/`PSCL` etc. — that finer split exists on some *football-data.co.uk* files but not here). `notes.txt` states directly: *"Betting odds for matches generally represent the most recent before play starts, as reported by oddsportal.com and the individual bookmakers."* In other words, what's stored is a de facto closing (or very near-closing) quote — the last price oddsportal.com had scraped before the match went live — not an opening line. This is the standard interpretation used across the tennis-forecasting literature (Sackmann, Kovalchik, every Kaggle mirror surveyed treats these as closing odds), but it is **not** a timestamped exchange-style close (no minute-level provenance per bookmaker); it's "whatever was last seen" during weekly-batch scraping. For a "beat the closing line" benchmark this is good enough and is what essentially the entire published literature uses, but it should be documented as an approximation, not a guarantee.

**Max/Avg columns.** Confirmed present for every match in both files: `MaxW`/`MaxL` (best price across the books oddsportal tracked) and `AvgW`/`AvgL` (their average). Useful as a market-consensus proxy independent of any single bookmaker's own margin.

**Licence and terms.** `terms.php` 404s — there is no dedicated terms page. The site's own `data.php` states: *"Tennis-Data maintains full copyright over the data files in their spreadsheet format."* That's the entirety of the explicit licence language found — no statement of permitted personal/non-commercial use, no redistribution terms, no API terms of service. In practice the data has been used freely in a large number of academic theses, papers and public Kaggle/GitHub projects (several found and cited below) without any evident enforcement action, so the de facto norm is "free to use for research/personal analysis, don't resell the raw files as your own product" — but that norm isn't backed by any published licence text, so dropshotverse should treat this as **research/personal-use-only by convention, not by contract**, and not build a commercial product directly on redistributed tennis-data.co.uk files.

**Ownership vs. the ATP's own rights JV — resolved, they are different entities.**
- "**Tennis Data Innovations (UK) Limited**" (Companies House #12974514, confirmed via `find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk`) was incorporated **26 October 2020**, registered in Wimbledon, London, SIC code 64209 (holding company). Public reporting (LinkedIn, PitchBook, ZoomInfo) is consistent: it is an independent joint venture formed in 2020 by the **ATP Tour and ATP Media** specifically to commercialise official ATP Tour + ATP Challenger Tour data and streaming rights, led by ex-Sportradar CEO David Lampitt, and covers 17,000+ matches/season across the main tour and Challenger tour.
- "**tennis-data.co.uk**" (the site scraped for this ticket) has existed since roughly 2000/2001 — predating the 2020 JV by two decades — and is registered to and run by **Joseph Buchdahl**, an independent betting-industry author/analyst who also runs the well-known **football-data.co.uk** (same house style, same "Data" branding, cross-linked in the site's own nav menu, and Buchdahl is independently documented across multiple sources — Pinnacle's own betting-resources site, sharpbetting.co.uk, High Stakes Publishing — as the person behind both).
- **Verdict: the name overlap is coincidental, not corporate.** tennis-data.co.uk is not owned by, licensed from, or affiliated with the ATP's official "Tennis Data Innovations" JV. That matters for the ticket in two ways: (1) nothing about tennis-data.co.uk's (thin) licence terms is governed by or backed by the ATP's official data-rights vehicle, so there's no implicit official blessing to lean on; (2) there is a longer-run *business* risk — the ATP's own commercial data JV, which explicitly covers Challenger-level data the free site doesn't have, could in principle assert IP/database-right claims that squeeze free scrape-based sites like this one — but that's a risk to the *source's longevity*, not a change to what's legally available today.

**Alternative free/near-free archives surveyed:**
- **Betfair Exchange historical data** (`historicdata.betfair.com`) — tennis markets available as timestamped JSON (gzipped TAR downloads) for every Exchange market since the Stream API launched in **2016**. A free "Basic" tier gives last-traded-price-per-minute (no volume, no full ladder); paid Advanced/Pro tiers add volume and full order-book depth. This is arguably the *conceptually* better benchmark the ticket flagged: exchange back/lay prices bracket the true probability without a fixed bookmaker margin baked in (there's still an effective spread/commission, but not overround in the fixed-odds sense). Trade-offs: only 10 years of coverage vs. tennis-data.co.uk's 25, requires a registered Betfair account, and requires building JSON/TAR-parsing tooling to get from tick data to a single match-level "closing price" — materially higher integration cost than a flat CSV.
- **Kaggle mirrors** — several found (`hakeem/atp-and-wta-tennis-data`, `ehallmar/a-large-tennis-dataset-for-atp-and-itf-betting` claiming 2M+ matches "for ATP and ITF betting", `edoardoba/atp-tennis-data`, `mexwell/ultimate-tennis-matches-dataset`). These are, on inspection of their own descriptions, re-hosted or lightly-merged copies of tennis-data.co.uk (+ Sackmann match data) — same underlying bookmaker coverage limits, and any "ITF" claim in a Kaggle title should be treated with suspicion (likely from oddsportal-level scraping of live current odds, not a genuine historical ITF/Challenger closing-odds archive). Licence terms are whatever the individual uploader claims, not authoritative.
- **BigDataBall** (bigdataball.com) — a paid subscription producing similar Excel odds+stats files for ATP/WTA; a commercial fallback if free coverage gaps ever matter, but has no free Challenger/ITF coverage either.
- **Oddsportal.com directly** — the actual upstream source tennis-data.co.uk scrapes from; it lists more bookmakers per match than the two tennis-data.co.uk retains. Scraping it directly could get closer to a true multi-book closing line, but means building and maintaining a scraper against a site that actively resists scraping — a real parallel project, not a "free archive" in the flat-file sense.
- **No free historical Challenger/ITF odds archive was found anywhere.** Current-day sportsbooks (FanDuel, BetMGM, etc.) do offer live Challenger/ITF markets today with thin liquidity and uneven per-book coverage, but there is no equivalent free historical CSV/API archive of these odds. If Challenger-level closing-line benchmarking is ever required, it is not achievable retroactively with any free source found — only prospectively, by scraping live sportsbook Challenger odds going forward.

### Part B — the join

**No published crosswalk table exists.** Checked Jeff Sackmann's own repos (`tennis_atp`, `tennis_wta`), the derivative `TML-Database`, `Ultimate Tennis Statistics`/`tennis-crystal-ball`, and the Kaggle mirrors above — none publishes a standalone, reusable tennis-data.co.uk ↔ Sackmann-player-ID crosswalk. Every project that needs this join rolls its own, usually as private pickled/hardcoded lookup tables, not published separately.

**Concrete example found and read: [`carltoews/tennis`](https://github.com/carltoews/tennis)**, specifically `notebooks/merge_datasets.ipynb` (fetched and read cell-by-cell from GitHub). It merges exactly the two sources this ticket is about — Sackmann's `tennis_atp` match data and tennis-data.co.uk's odds files — and documents its own failure modes up front ("bad player names", "bad match dates" — screenshots embedded in the notebook). Its cascading join strategy, concretely:

1. **Normalize first.** Both sides: strip, lowercase, and replace hyphens with spaces in tournament `Location`/`Winner`/`Loser` fields.
2. **Match tournaments before matches.** tennis-data.co.uk's tournament number (`ATP`/`WTA` column) resets every calendar year, while Sackmann's `tourney_id` is globally unique — so they aren't directly joinable. The notebook groups both sides by tournament, extracts per-tournament features (match count, min/max date), and joins on a **hand-curated `Location → tourney_name` lookup table** — of roughly 100 distinct tournaments, "about 60 have a direct correspondence" and the rest needed manual mapping (their example: `Location = "Paris"` in the odds file maps to `tourney_name = "Roland Garros"` in Sackmann's data). Match-count (a Grand Slam has 127 matches, a Masters 1000 has 55/47, etc.) is used as a sanity cross-check on each proposed tournament pairing, with an explicit warning printed if sizes disagree.
3. **Match rounds via draw-size-dependent lookup tables**, because "1st Round" in the odds file can correspond to `R128`, `R64`, or `R32` in Sackmann's data depending on how big that specific draw was — so four separate round-lookup tables are built (`round_lookup_small/medium/large/RR`) keyed off tournament size.
4. **Match players via a cascade, within (tournament, round):**
   - **Stage 1 — exact match** on the last name token of both winner and loser. Resolves the bulk of matches immediately.
   - **Stage 2 — substring match**: split every player name into whitespace tokens and check for *any* shared token between the two candidate name lists (handles compound/hyphenated surnames and token-order differences).
   - **Stage 3 — relax the round constraint** and repeat stage 2 (recovers cases where the round was miscoded on one side).
   - **Stage 4 — hardcoded manual patches** for known name-variant mismatches. Their literal example: `"delbonis"` vs. `"del bonis"` (Argentine player Federico Delbonís/Del Bonis spelled two different ways across the two sources).
   - **Stage 5 — final "dregs"**: whatever tiny remainder is left is resolved by sorting both leftover lists and pairing positionally, then a `find_winner()` function cross-checks and *corrects* winner/loser swaps by counting which of the two names appears across more rounds of that tournament in the odds data (whoever played more rounds actually won — a clever use of the round-progression structure itself as a tie-breaker).
5. Final pickle files (`matches.pkl`, `odds.pkl`) are saved keyed on a single shared integer key built from this cascade.

The notebook doesn't publish a final match-rate percentage, but the shape of the funnel it prints at each stage (`"A total of {N} matches down. {M} remain."`) is informative: stage 1 resolves the large majority, each subsequent stage recovers a small increment, and the final "dregs" needing fully manual resolution is a handful of rows per multi-year pull (2010–2016 in their case) — i.e. **very high (high-90s %) automatic match rate, with a small hand-patched residual**, not a clean 100%.

**Recommended join strategy for dropshotverse**, modelled directly on the above (this is exactly the cascade to replicate, not reinvent):
- Primary key: **(tournament, round, normalized surname pair)** rather than trying to match on full names or dates alone — dates are unreliable (tennis-data.co.uk's `Date` is sometimes the match date, sometimes the tournament start date for older years; Sackmann's `tourney_date` is always the tournament start date).
- Tournament identity: build a hand-checked `Location ↔ tourney_name` lookup (~100 entries, most tour-level tournaments recur every year with stable names) plus a match-count sanity check, rather than trusting name equality alone.
- Round identity: draw-size-dependent lookup tables, not a single fixed Round-name map.
- Player identity: exact-surname-match first, then token-substring fallback, then a small hand-maintained patch list for known transliteration/spelling variants (accented names, compound surnames, "de/del/van" particles) — expect to build and maintain this patch list ourselves since none is published anywhere.
- **Expected match rate: ~95–99% automatic at ATP/WTA tour level**, with the remaining 1–5% either hand-patched (à la "delbonis"/"del bonis") or accepted as dropped from the benchmark set. Retirements/walkovers (present in tennis-data.co.uk's `Comment` field) and any Challenger-adjacent qualifying rounds are the likeliest sources of residual mismatch.
- Secondary academic reference for the same problem: Sipko (2015), Imperial College MEng thesis *"Machine Learning for the Prediction of Professional Tennis Matches"*, cited by `carltoews/tennis` itself as the methodological precedent that first combined tennis-data.co.uk-style odds with Sackmann-style match statistics.

### Part C — the overround (de-vigging)

**Measured directly from the live-downloaded files** (not assumed): mean bookmaker overround in ATP 2024 was **5.17% for Bet365** and **2.61% for Pinnacle**; WTA 2024 was 5.17% (Bet365) and 2.88% (Pinnacle). This confirms Pinnacle's reputation in the literature as the "sharp", low-margin reference book, and is the practical reason to de-vig *Pinnacle's* price (`PSW`/`PSL`) as the primary series, not Bet365 or the `Avg` column.

**Standard methods, in the order the literature discusses them:**
1. **Proportional / multiplicative ("basic normalization")** — divide each implied probability (`1/odds`) by their sum. Simplest, but known to systematically mis-handle the **favourite–longshot bias**: bettors structurally overbet longshots, so a longshot's raw implied probability is inflated by more than the favourite's, and proportional splitting removes the margin equally in relative terms from both sides — over-correcting the favourite, under-correcting the longshot.
2. **Shin's method** (H.S. Shin, 1992, 1993) — models an assumed fraction `z` of "insider"/informed money and solves for the implied true probabilities net of both the bookmaker margin and this informed-trading distortion. For markets with more than two outcomes it requires an iterative numerical solve; **for exactly two outcomes — every tennis match — `z` can be computed analytically in closed form**, and (per the `mberk/shin` Python package's own documentation, which cites Clarke et al. directly) Shin's method is then mathematically **equivalent to the simpler "Additive" method** in the two-outcome case. This is a genuine computational simplification specific to tennis-shaped (two-outcome) markets.
3. **Power method** (Vovk & Zhadanov 2009; formalised for sports betting by **Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram, 2017**, *"Adjusting Bookmaker's Odds to Allow for Overround"*, American Journal of Sports Science 5(6)) — raise each side's implied probability to a fixed exponent `k` (found by 1-D root-finding so the powered values sum to 1). By construction this **never produces a probability outside [0,1]**, even when the adjustment is inverted/reapplied — both proportional normalization and Shin's method *can* produce out-of-range values under reversal, which the paper flags as a real defect. Because raising a small probability to a power `k>1` shrinks it proportionally more than a large one, the power method also naturally corrects favourite–longshot bias.
4. **Logarithmic method** — a less commonly used member of the same power-law family; not preferred in any of the literature surveyed.

**Which the literature actually recommends, and why this matters for a two-outcome market like tennis:**
- **Štrumbelj (2014, *International Journal of Forecasting* 30(4), 934–943)**, the most-cited general cross-sport comparison, found Shin's method produces more accurate probability forecasts (better calibration *and* resolution) than basic normalization across multiple sports/bookmakers, and that regression-based approaches, while calibrated, have poor resolution and "should not be used."
- **Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram (2017)** is the more directly relevant paper: **Stephanie Kovalchik is one of the most-published tennis-specific forecasting researchers** (the "GOAT of tennis win prediction" comparative study), and this paper tested basic/multiplicative, Shin, and their proposed power method across **three large sport-specific bookmaker datasets, one of which was tennis**. Their findings: the multiplicative method can produce invalid (<0 or >1) probabilities under reversal; **the power method universally outperformed the multiplicative method, and matched or beat Shin's method, across all three sports** including tennis.
- The two papers are not in conflict so much as answering slightly different questions (Shin vs. basic normalization in general, vs. power vs. Shin specifically) — but the tennis-specific, more recent paper (Clarke/Kovalchik/Ingram) is the one that should govern this ticket.

**Recommendation: use the power method as the primary de-vigging method, with Shin's method computed alongside as a secondary/validation cross-check.**
- Primary — **power method**, on the direct authority of Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram (2017), the paper written by tennis-forecasting specialists that explicitly tested tennis odds and found power ≥ Shin and power > proportional. It's cheap to compute for a two-outcome market (a single 1-D root-find per match), guarantees valid [0,1] probabilities even if the pipeline re-derives/re-applies implied probabilities repeatedly, and corrects for the favourite-longshot bias that a naive proportional split would leave in the benchmark — which matters directly for this ticket's stated purpose ("beating the market" claims are meaningless if the margin was removed wrongly): a proportional de-vig would make longshot upset picks look artificially better and favourite picks look artificially worse than the model actually deserves.
- Secondary — **Shin's method**, computed alongside for validation, since (a) it's the more established/cited method in the general (non-tennis-specific) literature, (b) for a two-outcome market it's essentially free to compute in closed form (no iteration needed, per the `mberk/shin` package), and (c) meaningful divergence between the power-method and Shin-method probabilities on a given match is itself a useful diagnostic — a larger implied `z` (informed-money fraction) from Shin's solve flags a match where the market may have been less efficient, which is exactly the kind of match a "beat the closing line" claim should be scrutinised more carefully on.
- Apply de-vigging to **Pinnacle's price (`PSW`/`PSL`)** as the primary reference series, not Bet365 or the archive's own `Avg` column — Pinnacle's measured overround in the live 2024 files (≈2.6–2.9%) is roughly half Bet365's (≈5.2%), consistent with its industry reputation as the standard "sharp"/reference book, and a lower true margin means less of the de-vig method's own assumptions get stress-tested by the correction itself.

### Deliverable — verdict

- **Closing-line benchmarking is viable for ATP and WTA main tour**, back to 2001 (ATP odds) / 2007 (WTA odds), using tennis-data.co.uk's `PSW`/`PSL` (Pinnacle) and `B365W`/`B365L` (Bet365) columns as de facto closing prices ("most recent before play starts," per the site's own documentation). Coverage is free, current through this week, updated weekly, and >99.5% complete at the main-tour level in the files actually inspected.
- **Challenger/ITF has no odds coverage at all** in this archive, and no free alternative archive with genuine historical Challenger/ITF odds was found anywhere. Live sportsbooks offer thin, uneven Challenger/ITF markets today, but there's no way to backfill history for that tier for free. Any closing-line benchmark must be scoped to ATP + WTA main tour only.
- **Join strategy**: normalize names → match tournaments via a hand-checked `Location↔tourney_name` lookup plus match-count sanity checks → match rounds via draw-size-dependent lookup tables → match players via exact-surname match, then token-substring fallback, then a hand-maintained patch list for known spelling variants — directly modelled on the public `carltoews/tennis` (`merge_datasets.ipynb`) implementation, since no published crosswalk table exists anywhere. **Expected match rate: ~95–99% automatic**, with a small hand-patched or accepted-drop residual.
- **Recommended de-vig method: the power method** (Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram, 2017), applied to Pinnacle's price as the primary reference book, with Shin's method computed in parallel as a free (closed-form, two-outcome) validation cross-check.
