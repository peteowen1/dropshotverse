# Map: dropshotverse tennis rating & simulation engine

Charted 2026-08-27.

## Destination

**Revised 2026-08-27 on measured evidence — see ticket 10.**

A written build spec for **dropshotverse** (two repos: `dropshot` + `dropshotdata`), plus **one throwaway prototype** demonstrating three things:

1. **Calibration parity with the market** — the model's stated probabilities match observed frequencies as well as the de-vigged closing line does, on held-out seasons. Not beating the line; matching it.
2. **Live in-match win probability**, updating per point in closed form.
3. **Draw simulation** producing each player's odds of winning an event.

Items 2 and 3 are the actual justification for the architecture: they are what a point-level model can do and a match-outcome Elo structurally cannot.

We stop when the spec is written and the prototype has numbers attached. Building the production package is a separate effort.

### Why this changed

The original destination was "reaches or beats bookmaker closing-line calibration". Ticket 10 measured the gap and it is not close: a surface-adjusted Elo trails Pinnacle by **0.026 log-loss**, and where it disagrees with the market it is right only **40.5%** of the time. Ingram's published point-level model trailed FiveThirtyEight's Elo by 0.006 — so a point model of comparable quality would still lose to the closing line by a wide margin.

Keeping "beat the market" would have meant either a failed project or a quietly redefined success criterion months from now. Better to redraw it once, on evidence, at the point the evidence arrived.

## Notes

**Domain.** Tennis analytics. One engine must serve three products:
1. Pre-match win probability for any pairing
2. Tournament draw simulation (each player's odds of winning an event)
3. Live in-match win probability, updating as points are played

Plus two ranking outputs: current form (both tours, by surface) and an all-time GOAT list.

**Architecture decided in charting (2026-08-27).** The four options put to Pete were mis-framed as rivals; they are layers of one model and all four are in:
- *What* is modelled: point-level serve/return skill. Every point is a Bernoulli whose bias depends on server skill, returner skill, surface.
- *Covariate*: surface adjustment sits inside that bias.
- *Estimation*: Bayesian state-space filter over time.
- *Elo*: not a rival design — it is the cheap online approximation of that same filter (cf. Glicko). It serves as the fast path and as a benchmark.

**Two-speed shape.** Slow layer = Bayesian state-space, refit in batch, produces rankings and is the source of truth. Fast layer = closed-form update cheap enough to run per-point in a Cloudflare Worker, seeded from the slow layer's posterior.

**The coverage trick that makes this feasible — NOW IN DOUBT, see ticket 12.** Charting assumed that because scorelines constrain underlying point-win rates, the point-level model could be fit on *match scorelines* for the bulk of history. Ticket 05 found **no published paper does this** — the literature works from point counts or panel data instead. The likely reprieve is that the recovered Sackmann schema carries per-match serve statistics (`svpt`, `1stWon`, `2ndWon`, `bpSaved`…), which *are* point counts and are exactly what Klaassen & Magnus use. Ticket 12 must establish what fraction of matches actually carry them before this assumption is relied on.

- [11 — Failure modes, and how differently do men's and women's tennis behave?](issues/11-serve-dominance-failure-modes.md) — **Serve-dominance gap quantified and its consequence is narrower than feared.** ATP 64.0% vs WTA 58.3% service points won (Xiao, Osman & Wang 2026, Slam finalists 2019-2023, p<0.001 at every major, surface does not explain it); hold rates 81.2% vs 70.0%. The derivative, computed directly from ticket 05's recursions and numerically verified: `dP_game/dp` is **26% steeper at WTA's operating point** (2.206 vs 1.753) because ATP's higher `p` sits on the flattening tail of the S-curve — but `dP_match/dp` is only **~5% steeper** (5.221 vs 4.981). So the sensitivity gap lives at the point→game step, not game→match. Best-of-5 amplifies point-error into match-error by ~25% over best-of-3 at both tours. The gap is driven mostly by **first**-serve outcomes; second-serve differences are significant only at the Australian Open — which argues for splitting first/second-serve parameters by tour rather than one blended rate. Retirements: field-wide practice is discard-without-justification; no paper treats retirement as informative. i.i.d. points: not-quite-i.i.d.; Paserman (2010/2023) sizes the importance effect at a 50%→75-80% swing, similar for both genders (correcting a "women choke more" oversimplification). **Recommendation:** neither pooled nor fully separate — one Ingram-style hierarchical fit with tour as a grouping factor on the serve/return layer (separate `alpha`/`beta` and variance per tour), sharing the recursion, surface effects and importance structure. **NOT ESTABLISHED:** altitude/ball-type effects, best-of-5 skill transfer, decade-by-decade era drift.
- [12 — How do we actually estimate point-win rates?](issues/12-scoreline-inversion-gap.md) — **Measured, and the news is good.** Serve-stat coverage across the archive: ATP tour 79.8→93.9% since 1990, ATP Challenger 95.6%/99.5% (2010s/2020s), WTA tour 0% pre-2000 then 85.6%/95.2%, **pre-1991 zero everywhere**. So the modern era fits on point counts (published, low risk) and only the deep history — where the GOAT list lives — needs inversion. **Identifiability then tested computationally rather than assumed:** from one match a 6-4 6-4 puts the MLE at the grid boundary, wildly wrong — *per-match inversion is hopeless, which is likely why it is unpublished.* But across a panel it recovers truth: ~10 sets gets close, **150 sets recovers p1=0.65 vs a true 0.64 with a ±0.03 interval.** Identification comes from repetition, not from any single scoreline. A global normalisation is still needed (only `serve_i − return_j` enters a match), so fix mean return skill at zero as Bradley-Terry does. **The GOAT list is feasible**, with genuinely wider uncertainty bands pre-1991 that must be shown rather than hidden.
- [13 — Snapshot and freeze the base historical archive](issues/13-snapshot-base-archive.md) — **Done. The foundation is now ours, not a stranger's.** 473 files fetched from each of the GitHub and HuggingFace mirrors (~1.5 GB on disk), frozen at `dropshotdata/`, with a full manifest (per-file sha256, size, row count, mirror) at `dropshotdata/manifest/`. **All 465 CSV data files are byte-for-byte identical across both mirrors** — zero mismatches — which is strong independent corroboration that the snapshot is a faithful copy of Sackmann's data rather than someone's edit. Totals: **1,774,873 singles matches** (ATP 988,216 / WTA 786,657), 57,714 unique players. No git repo created, nothing pushed or published — publishing to a Release still needs Pete's explicit go-ahead.
- [01 — What historical tennis data actually survives?](issues/01-historical-data-archaeology.md) — **Recoverable, but as three stitched layers, not one source.** *Base:* `Aneeshers/tennis-sackmann-archive` (GitHub + HuggingFace) — a frozen full 49-column Sackmann-schema snapshot, ATP+WTA at every level (tour/challenger/futures/qualifying/doubles) plus slam point-by-point, cut ~2026-05-25. Corroborated two independent ways: byte-identical copies re-vendored into unrelated ML repos, and Software Heritage's last successful `tennis_atp` crawl (2026-05-08) landing just before the cutoff. *Live ATP patch:* `stats.tennismylife.org` `/api/data-files` manifest, 1-3 days fresh, uniquely covers current ATP Challenger and Qualifying. *Live cross-tour patch:* `LuckyLoser91/TennisCourtLog` / tennis-data.co.uk — freshest of all, both tours, but tour-level only with a gutted 13-column schema and **no player IDs**. **Risks:** no source anywhere covers WTA Challenger/ITF live (structural gap — TennisMyLife's manifest has 49 ATP challenger files and zero WTA); TennisMyLife's licensing is self-contradictory (MIT badge vs research-only README); stitching the live layer onto the ID-keyed base needs bespoke name resolution. **Confirmed dead:** Kaggle `hakeem/atp-and-wta-tennis-data` (stale since 2020), `mcekovic/uts-database` Docker image (stale since 2021).
- [04 — Is the bookmaker odds archive good enough to be our benchmark, and can we join it?](issues/04-odds-archive-and-joining.md) — **Viable for main tour, zero coverage below it.** tennis-data.co.uk is free, weekly, ATP odds from 2001 and WTA from 2007, current through 2026-08-24, >99.5% complete on main tour — and is run independently by Joseph Buchdahl, *unrelated* to the ATP's similarly-named Tennis Data Innovations JV (Companies House #12974514). Only two individual-bookmaker columns survive in recent seasons, `B365W/L` and `PSW/L`, plus `Max`/`Avg`; the nine other books are legacy-only, so **the loader must treat the schema as year-dependent**. No opening/closing split, but the odds are documented as "most recent before play starts" — de facto closing, and how the whole literature treats them. **Challenger/ITF has zero odds coverage anywhere free** (confirmed from the literal Series/Tier values: ATP250 upward only). **Join:** no published crosswalk exists; the working pattern is `carltoews/tennis` `merge_datasets.ipynb` — normalise names, match tournaments via a hand-built Location↔tourney_name lookup with match-count checks, rounds via draw-size lookup, then cascade player matching (exact surname → token substring → hand-patched variants). Expect ~95-99% automatic. **De-vig:** power method (Clarke, Kovalchik & Ingram 2017, tennis-specific) as primary, Shin's as a secondary check; de-vig against **Pinnacle**, whose measured margin is ~2.6-2.9% vs Bet365's ~5.2%.
- [05 — What published point-level tennis model should we base the engine on?](issues/05-point-model-literature.md) — **Items 1-5 established from primary sources; item 6 not.** Closed-form recursions written out and verified: game `P(p)=p⁴(15−34p+28p²−8p³)/(1−2p+2p²)`, tiebreak as an 8×8 DP with ABBA alternation and a geometric 6-6 tail, set via DP over games-won, match as a binomial combination of set probabilities; final-set format table 1970-2022 confirmed (the Wimbledon 12-12 and pre-2019 advantage variants are derived by analogy, not found pre-published — flagged). Ingram (2019) fully parameterised (binomial likelihood, logistic link on server/returner skill, surface, tournament, Gaussian random-walk time dynamics; Stan/NUTS; code at `github.com/martiningram/tennis_bayes_point_based`; **68.8% / 0.592 log-loss**). Kovalchik (2016) Table 4 read from the primary PDF: **BCM 72% / 0.55**, 538-career Elo 70% / 0.59, opponent-adjusted 67% / 0.63 — ATP only, no WTA split. Elo-as-Bayesian-filter via Glickman (1999), Glicko-2, TrueSkill, Szczecinski & Tihon (2021). **Key negative finding:** no published paper inverts *bare scorelines* through the full Markov likelihood — everyone uses point counts or panel data (Klaassen & Magnus, Barnett & Clarke). See ticket 12.
- [06 — How does the ITG live-model pattern work, and what must a tennis model export to slot in?](issues/06-itg-live-delivery-pattern.md) — **The closed-form path is viable, and no ML runtime is needed.** The Worker's real contract is "fetch a small JSON blob from R2 once per isolate, run JS arithmetic on it per request" — XGBoost is what the football/AFL models happen to implement, not a platform requirement. `worker/src/live-wp-calibration.js` already ships a non-ML precedent: 5 scalars in R2 plus ~6 lines of arithmetic, with a hard never-throw/identity-fallback rule to copy. Tennis is architecturally identical, with per-player serve/return parameters instead of 5 scalars and a Markov recursion instead of a sigmoid — and *cheaper* than what ships today (AFL's live WP walks 152 XGBoost trees per row; the recursion is a few hundred float ops per poll tick). `live-poller.js` and `live-badge.js` are already sport-agnostic and need zero changes, just `sport: "tennis"` / `prefix: "ts"` config. Artifact: flat JSON, ~5-10 numbers per player, low hundreds of KB full-slate or under 1KB per match, published by a manual `wrangler r2 object put` exactly as torp does. **Does not transfer:** all clock-shaped code (quarter/half-time features, stoppage clocks, minutes-remaining leverage weights, possession-chain models). No blocker.
- [03 — What free live tennis data exists, and can it legally sit behind a public site?](issues/03-free-live-sources.md) — **None is defensible.** Sofascore and Flashscore are the only free sources with real live point-by-point, and both ToS explicitly ban the automated extraction required; no non-commercial carve-out exists in either. ATP and WTA sold *exclusive* live-data rights (Sportradar to 2029; Stats Perform), a category rights holders actively litigate. Every open-source scraper is dead, stale, or post-match-aggregate only. Forces ticket 07 toward a paid feed.
